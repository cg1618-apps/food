# A category carries its own notes

The owner's rule draws the line between the two tables: **an ingredient is
something bought or written on a recipe line as itself** (醬油, which also has
varieties); **a category is a family name whose members are bought but which
is never itself bought or cited** (雞肉, under 肉類, whose members are 雞腿 and
雞胸肉). Notes about a family - how to pick fresh chicken, how long it keeps -
need a home: on one member they are hidden from the others, and copied onto
each they drift. So a category has a `description`.

Rejected: **making 雞肉 an ingredient to hang the notes on.** It would be a row
never bought or cited, which the rule says an ingredient is not, and it would
appear in the typeahead and the stock list as if it could be.

**One free-text column, not the ingredient's four.** An ingredient splits its
notes by the moment of an errand they are read at; a family's notes are short
and few, and four boxes per category would be four empty boxes.

**Shown where the family is browsed**: above the ingredient library's list
when the category is chosen. A category is not a page of its own.
